Starting a Medical Writing Career

Medical writing translates scientific, clinical and regulatory information into clear documents for defined audiences. The type of writing, required expertise and review process vary by role and organization.

Common medical writing areas

  • Regulatory writing: clinical study reports, protocols, investigator brochures, summaries and submission-related documents.
  • Pharmacovigilance writing: aggregate safety reports, signal evaluation documents, risk management documents and safety narratives.
  • Scientific and publication writing: manuscripts, abstracts, posters, review articles and congress materials.
  • Medical communications: educational content, scientific slide decks and medical information materials.

Skills employers may look for

  • Strong grammar, concise scientific communication and careful proofreading.
  • Ability to interpret clinical and scientific evidence without overstating conclusions.
  • Understanding of study design, statistics, medical terminology and relevant guidelines.
  • Accurate referencing, document formatting, version control and collaboration with subject-matter experts.

How to build experience

  • Strengthen fundamentals in clinical research, pharmacology and evidence appraisal.
  • Practice summarizing published studies while preserving study design, population, outcomes and limitations.
  • Create sample work using public, non-confidential information and clearly label it as a sample.
  • Learn the document conventions and guidelines relevant to the specific writing track.
  • Seek supervised internships, entry-level roles or legitimate project opportunities.

Portfolio and professional conduct

  • Never include confidential employer, client, patient or unpublished study information in a portfolio.
  • Use public sources and cite them accurately.
  • Keep a record of your role in each sample and do not claim authorship or review activities you did not perform.
  • Tailor your resume to the document types and therapeutic areas relevant to the vacancy.
Important: This article is for general career education. Role eligibility, professional registration, regulatory requirements and employer processes can vary. Confirm current requirements with the relevant employer or official authority.
